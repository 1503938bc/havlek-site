A Health Plan’s 20% Efficiency Gain: An AI Operations Business Case

HCLTech’s October 2026 health plan case connects AI knowledge assist, document processing, and workflow redesign to reported gains in efficiency and quality.

What happens when a service team can find the right procedure faster and paper forms no longer need to be handled entirely by hand? In an October 13, 2026 case study, HCLTech describes an operations overhaul for an unnamed regional Blue Cross Blue Shield health plan. The provider reports a 20% efficiency gain enabled by generative AI knowledge assist and a 33% reduction in turnaround time for Medicare enrollments and plan changes using intelligent optical character recognition (OCR).

The figures are reported by the implementation vendor, and the client is not named. The study does not disclose the measurement period, baseline, sample size, costs, or an independent audit. It also describes a broader transition between service providers, so the outcomes cannot all be assigned to AI alone. Even with those limits, the case offers a practical view of how to connect AI to work that a business already measures.

Start With the Work, Not the Model

The health plan faced fragmented processes, manual work allocation, aging tools, and procedures scattered across SharePoint. According to HCLTech, those conditions made it harder to meet service and quality targets and handle paper-heavy enrollments. Employees had to locate the right information while managing queues and resolving requests.

The implementation combined a central process-management layer for routing and reporting, an AI-supported knowledge and learning system, document processing, and robotic process automation (RPA). These are different tools doing different jobs. The point is not that one chatbot fixed the whole operation; the provider says it redesigned how work, information, and automation came together.

A useful AI business case begins with a specific queue, a known source of delay, and a way to measure both speed and quality.

Make Knowledge Accessible and Maintainable

HCLTech says the knowledge system digitized more than 1,500 standard operating procedures across 12 functional areas, with document and change management. Its generative AI agent assist provides procedure-backed responses and suggested next actions to staff. The reported 20% efficiency gain is associated with that knowledge assist; the case does not explain precisely how efficiency was calculated.

For any organization with complex policies, the lesson is to treat the source material as part of the product. Assign owners to procedures, retire outdated versions, control access, and make it easy for staff to check the underlying source. A faster answer is only useful when it is based on the right policy for the task and can be corrected when that policy changes.

Separate Document Processing From Broader Automation

The vendor reports that intelligent OCR helped Medicare enrollments and plan changes achieve a 60% efficiency gain and a 33% reduction in turnaround time. It separately attributes 20%–25% cost optimization to scaled RPA-led automation, with emerging agentic capabilities contributing incrementally. It describes those agentic initiatives as in progress, rather than as the sole cause of the published results.

That distinction matters when planning an investment. Document extraction can reduce re-keying, while rules-based bots can move data through predictable steps; generative AI can help staff find and use procedures. Each has different costs, error modes, and review requirements. Map the handoffs before assuming that automating one step will improve the whole service.

Protect Quality While Measuring the Business Case

HCLTech also reports approximately 60% fewer manual errors and a 20%–30% quality improvement across functions. Those are broad transformation outcomes, not proof that a particular model caused the improvement. The study does not provide enough detail to reproduce the calculations or predict a comparable result elsewhere.

A practical pilot should establish the pre-change baseline for a single workflow, then compare turnaround time, first-pass accuracy, rework, staff review time, and cost per completed request. Track exceptions and audit samples alongside speed. Where a request affects coverage or other consequential decisions, design an explicit human review and escalation path, and limit access to sensitive information.

For context, CMS’s Medicare enrollment guidance describes eligibility and enrollment requirements and updates for 2027. A production system needs an accountable process for keeping its approved knowledge current; the case study does not establish that this specific implementation follows any particular CMS update.

The Havlek Takeaway

Start with a recurring workflow where slow information retrieval or document handling creates measurable rework. Connect approved knowledge, use the appropriate automation for each step, and give an owner responsibility for quality and exceptions. Calculate savings against implementation and operating costs before scaling. The opportunity is a better-managed service process—not a percentage borrowed from another organization’s case study.
