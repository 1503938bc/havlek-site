LoanPro’s 15% Shorter Calls: An AI Loan-Servicing Business Case

LoanPro’s production servicing assistant shows how an evaluated, human-supervised AI workflow can turn fragmented account data into faster customer conversations.

The useful AI business cases are increasingly the ones that do not start with a blank chat box. They start with a stubborn, expensive workflow. LoanPro, a loan-management platform, has deployed a production assistant for servicing teams that pulls together borrower context and recommends next steps during customer calls. In a November 17 AWS case study co-written with LoanPro, the company reported up to a 15% reduction in customer call times, nearly 8% fewer internal tickets, and account summaries delivered in under 15 seconds.

The results are vendor- and company-reported, so they should be treated as an operating case study rather than independent proof of universal ROI. Still, the design is instructive. LoanPro did not ask an AI model to replace a servicing professional. It targeted the first minutes of a call, when an agent must manually assemble account history, loan terms, prior interactions, and available workflow actions across multiple screens.

Start With the Context-Gathering Tax

According to the case study, servicing agents had been spending 18–20% of each call orienting themselves to the account—roughly one to two and a half minutes and five to seven clicks. The assistant consolidates that work into a one-click account summary, then offers a conversational interface for follow-up questions.

That is a better starting point than “where can we add an agent?” It identifies a recurring delay that has a known owner, a predictable sequence of data lookups, and a clear customer consequence. Faster orientation gives agents more time for the part of the interaction that requires judgment: explaining options, handling exceptions, and resolving the borrower’s actual problem.

The strongest AI opportunity is often not an autonomous decision. It is the repeated moment where capable people lose time reconstructing context before they can make one.

Connect to the System of Record, Not a Copy of It

LoanPro’s assistant reaches live account data through existing APIs. The reported implementation brings together eight API endpoints and roughly 1,400 fields per account, with specialized agents for retrieval, recommendation, and response composition. In practical terms, the workflow is useful because it operates where the work already happens: inside the servicing portal, not in a separate experimental interface.

That is an important architecture choice for business leaders. An isolated AI pilot can make an impressive answer without changing the work. A production workflow needs access to current data, an explicit path to the next action, and a boundary around which systems it may touch. The more sensitive the decision, the more important it is to keep those boundaries visible.

Evaluation and Governance Are Part of the Product

Lending makes the controls especially concrete. The case study says LoanPro created a benchmark of 150 subject-matter-expert-labeled accounts and held the assistant to a 99% accuracy target for retrieval, summary, and action selection. It also describes role-based access controls, audit logging, named-user attribution, and policy guardrails before outputs reach a servicing agent.

These are not administrative extras. They are what let the team use AI with borrower information while preserving accountability. The service professional remains responsible for the conversation; the system accelerates the preparation and provides a consistent starting point. That design also makes failure observable: teams can inspect a recommendation, compare it against the account record, correct it, and improve the evaluation set.

Measure Capacity and Customer Experience Together

LoanPro’s most useful metric is call time, but it is not the only one. The company also reports reduced ticket volume and more consistent servicing quality. That combination matters. A shorter call is not a win if it simply shifts work to another queue or makes a borrower less likely to get a complete answer.

For a similar rollout, establish a baseline for handling time, transfer or escalation rate, resolution quality, rework, compliance exceptions, and customer satisfaction. Then run the workflow long enough to capture both routine and complex cases. Treat capacity created by AI as an outcome to be reinvested—into better service, backlogs, or higher-value work—not a number that disappears into a slide.

The Havlek Takeaway

LoanPro’s case is a good reminder that governed AI can create real operating leverage in regulated work without pretending that human judgment is optional. Find the repeated context-gathering task before an important decision. Connect trusted data to the existing workflow, test against expert-labeled cases, log the work, and measure the downstream business result. That is how an AI assistant becomes a service improvement rather than another disconnected tool.
