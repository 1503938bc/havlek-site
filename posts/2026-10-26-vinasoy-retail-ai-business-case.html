Vinasoy’s 20% Out-of-Stock Reduction: An AI Retail Operations Business Case

Vinasoy’s retail-monitoring rollout shows how AI can make shelf data timely enough to improve in-stock performance—when it is connected to a clear operating response.

Retailers and consumer-goods companies rarely lose sales because nobody collected shelf data. They lose sales because the signal arrives after the shelf problem has already become a missed purchase. That is why a recent Vinasoy rollout is a useful AI business case: it links faster retail monitoring to a concrete commercial metric.

In a September 29, 2026 AWS release, the Vietnamese packaged soy milk producer said a generative-AI retail display monitoring system reduced its out-of-stock rate by 20% in two months. The company says monitoring coverage rose to more than 70% of distribution outlets per month, while display-scoring time fell from more than 20 days to under two days.

These are company and partner-reported results, not independently audited findings. The release does not provide the baseline number of stock-outs, costs, profit impact, or a controlled comparison. Still, its operating logic is sound: improve the speed and reach of a high-frequency signal, then give commercial teams enough time to act on it.

The Value Is in the Shorter Decision Loop

Before the deployment, Vinasoy says its sales teams waited weeks for display-compliance data. In the reported new process, the system automates retail display monitoring across the distribution network. The release says coverage expanded by more than four times and checks moved from monthly to weekly. That changes the work from retrospective reporting into a management loop: detect, prioritize, correct, and verify.

For an FMCG business, an empty shelf is not a dashboard problem. It can be an availability, replenishment, execution, or data-quality problem. AI is useful here only when it helps distinguish and route those cases more quickly. A team still needs a clear owner, a response deadline, and a way to close the loop at the store.

The business case is not “we can inspect more shelves.” It is “we can see exceptions early enough to recover a sale.”

Build Around an Existing Commercial Measure

The reported 20% improvement is more useful than a generic productivity claim because it points to an operating outcome: fewer unavailable products. That makes it possible to ask the right questions before copying the approach. Which product-store combinations are most costly to miss? What is the current time between observation and intervention? Which exceptions can a field team actually resolve?

Start with a narrow category or territory and measure the baseline: out-of-stock rate, display-compliance rate, time to score, time to assign, time to resolve, and replenishment accuracy. Include a quality check for false alerts. If the detection system flags a problem that does not exist, the saved reporting time can quickly become field-team waste.

Pair Detection With a Response System

A monitoring model does not replenish a shelf by itself. The important implementation detail is the handoff: alerts need to reach the right sales, merchandising, distributor, or retailer contact with enough context to decide what to do. That typically means store, SKU, evidence, severity, current inventory information where available, and a named next action.

Vinasoy says the monitoring project is part of a broader effort using data analytics for sales management, demand forecasting, and distribution optimization. The same release says a sales report for management meetings dropped from roughly three days to about five minutes. Those are separate reported outcomes, but they illustrate an important pattern: the best value usually comes when operational data is connected to the decisions people already need to make.

Use Vendor Claims as a Starting Point, Not a Forecast

The results were published by AWS and describe a solution built with its partner Renova Cloud. They are evidence that a deployment produced an observed result in one context—not proof that another company will see the same 20% reduction. Product mix, retail relationships, store coverage, image quality, incentives, and supply constraints all affect the outcome.

For a credible investment case, calculate the value of recovered availability against the full cost of implementation, data capture, integrations, review, and change management. Run a phased comparison where possible. Most importantly, make the success condition operational: a specific category, a measured reduction in stock-outs, and a documented response process when the system finds an exception.

The Havlek Takeaway

AI creates retail value when it compresses the gap between what is happening on the shelf and what the business can do about it. Do not begin with a visual AI pilot. Begin with a costly, measurable exception and design the ownership, evidence, and response path around it. Faster visibility becomes business value only when it reliably changes the next action.
