Crowell & Moring's 2 Million AI Interactions: A 2026 Business Case for AI Adoption in Legal Services

Crowell & Moring's latest legal AI results matter because they show something most enterprise AI rollouts still cannot: sustained weekly usage by high-value professionals, measurable client-work improvements, and governance strong enough to support scale.

One of the clearest recent business cases for enterprise AI adoption did not come from a software company. It came from Crowell & Moring, which on July 22, 2026 published six-month results from its firmwide rollout of Legora, a collaborative AI platform for legal work. The headline numbers are unusually strong: more than 2 million interactions, 82% of the entire firm using the platform, 91% of its 700-plus attorneys onboarded, and nearly 70% of those attorneys using it at least weekly.

That matters because legal services are among the hardest environments in which to prove AI value. The work is expensive, document-heavy, high-risk, and client-specific. If a large law firm can move AI from pilot curiosity into routine weekly practice across most of its attorneys, it says something important about where AI adoption becomes commercially credible for the broader knowledge economy.

The strongest AI business cases are no longer about whether people tried the tool. They are about whether elite professionals changed how they work every week.

Why This Case Stands Out

Most AI announcements still lean on vague language such as "innovation," "transformation," or "future of work." Crowell's case is more useful because it reports actual operating signals. Weekly usage across a majority of attorneys is materially harder to fake than license counts or launch-day adoption. It suggests the platform is embedded in real client work rather than sitting on the shelf after an enthusiastic pilot.

The firm also provides a clearer implementation story than most enterprise releases. Crowell did not start from zero. It had already built an innovation department in 2021 and launched its proprietary CrowellAI platform in early 2024. Legora was then selected after evaluation by the innovation team and more than 40 pilot attorneys who were already power users with advanced client use cases to test. That sequence matters: mature internal capability reduced the odds of AI becoming a disconnected procurement exercise.

Just as important, the rollout was accompanied by discipline. Crowell says deployment was accelerated through mandatory firmwide AI training, tool-specific onboarding, practice-specific workshops, internal reporting across AI tools, and governance requirements inside the platform. This is exactly the pattern most enterprises miss. AI adoption works better when training, reporting, and guardrails are part of the operating design instead of compliance cleanup after the fact.

Where The Business Value Shows Up

The first layer is research and synthesis speed. Crowell partner Jason Crawford said the firm can now give clients faster, more comprehensive advice backed by specific scenarios that otherwise would have taken days to uncover. That is not just time savings. In a premium professional-services model, faster scenario analysis changes what kind of counsel can be delivered at all.

The second layer is service-line expansion. Crawford also said the added speed and depth opened the door to new offerings around risk assessment and compliance. This is a stronger business signal than simple efficiency. When AI allows a firm to sell a better or broader service, the ROI conversation moves from labor savings to growth and margin quality.

The third layer is better leverage of junior talent. Associate Christian Lichtenberger described being able to operate at a higher level across corporate matters, with clients effectively receiving higher value from a lower-cost resource. In professional services, that shift is economically meaningful. If junior and mid-level staff can contribute closer to senior quality more quickly, firms improve both delivery capacity and margin structure.

The fourth layer is better performance in high-stakes matters. Health Care Litigation partner Shelley Rosenberg said the platform helped teams access key evidence faster and test ideas more quickly in time-sensitive work, including two recent arbitrations the firm won. Patent associate Elissa Tenenbaum similarly described faster preparation during trial work and more nimble responses against a larger opposing team. Those are not guarantees of legal outcomes, but they are credible signals that AI improved the speed and strategic depth of execution where it matters most.

Why Executives Outside Legal Should Care

Legal work is a useful proxy for many other high-value knowledge workflows. The same characteristics show up in consulting, insurance, compliance, financial analysis, healthcare administration, procurement, and enterprise sales operations: large document volumes, fragmented institutional knowledge, expensive expert time, and pressure to produce better decisions faster.

Crowell's case suggests three broader lessons. First, AI works best when it is paired with proprietary knowledge, not treated as a generic chat box. Second, the strongest returns appear when advanced users shape rollout early and prove real workflow fit. Third, adoption at scale depends less on raw model quality than on whether the organization builds onboarding, governance, and use-case design around the tool.

This is also a reminder that "successful AI adoption" is often better measured by durable behavior than by a single percentage improvement. More than 2 million interactions and weekly usage by a majority of attorneys show repeated operational reliance. That is a stronger indicator of business value than isolated benchmark claims.

What Other Businesses Should Copy

  • Measure weekly workflow usage, not just seat activation. Real business value shows up when professionals return to the tool as part of client work.
  • Run advanced-user pilots before broad rollout. Crowell used more than 40 power users to test high-value use cases before scaling firmwide.
  • Combine AI with your proprietary knowledge base. The commercial payoff increases when AI can reason across internal precedents, documents, and domain context.
  • Build governance into the rollout path. Mandatory training, onboarding, internal reporting, and practice-level workshops make adoption safer and more durable.
  • Look for revenue and leverage gains, not only labor savings. The best enterprise AI cases improve service breadth, strategic quality, and the effective output of skilled teams.

The Havlek Takeaway

Crowell & Moring's July 2026 results are one of the more credible recent AI adoption cases because they combine recency, scale, and behavioral evidence. More than 2 million interactions in six months, 91% attorney adoption, and roughly 70% weekly usage suggest the tool is not an experiment anymore. It has become part of how work gets done.

The broader business lesson is straightforward: AI becomes commercially serious when it is tied to proprietary knowledge, tested by expert users, governed centrally, and measured in workflow behavior that repeats. That pattern is portable far beyond law firms. Any business built on expensive expertise should study it.
