Sophos' 96% Investigation-Time Cut: A Governed AI Cybersecurity Business Case

Sophos' new AI-agent case shows the commercial value of compressing repeatable security investigations while preserving clear human authority over consequential response actions.

AI adoption becomes commercially compelling when it speeds up a constrained, high-volume workflow without blurring accountability. In an October 9, 2026 customer story, cybersecurity firm Sophos reports that cases using its AI agents now average 89 seconds to investigate and respond, compared with roughly 38 minutes before—a reported 96% reduction in investigation time. Sophos also says AI resolves 52% of its Managed Detection and Response (MDR) cases end-to-end.

These are company-reported results in a vendor-published case study, not an independent audit. They should not be read as a universal benchmark for security teams or as proof that autonomous response is appropriate everywhere. But the deployment is still useful because it makes the operating model visible: the AI has a bounded job, it uses domain-specific context, and humans retain authority where the action could matter most.

The Valuable Unit of Work Was a Case, Not “Security AI”

Sophos Fusion combines telemetry from more than 500 third-party integrations and Sophos products. The company says those sensors generate trillions of events each day, which it reduces to roughly 1,000 to 2,000 cases for its nine security operations centres to investigate. That funnel is the important design choice. The AI is not asked to interpret every raw event in the world; it works on a case that has already been assembled into a meaningful investigation unit.

For each case, Sophos describes an investigation agent that gathers customer context, detections, indicators of compromise, and relevant threat intelligence. A planning model then creates a plan–execute–review loop, which produces a summary and recommended response actions for an analyst. Other agents may carry out parts of the response within the boundaries Sophos has set.

Choose a repeatable decision package with known inputs, an explicit handoff, and a measurable turnaround time—not a broad mandate to “automate security.”

Context and Workflow Matter More Than a Chat Window

Security operations are a poor fit for generic, disconnected prompting. The right answer depends on the customer environment, the actual detection, current threat intelligence, and a playbook that reflects what the team is allowed to do. Sophos pairs the models with its own threat intelligence, response playbooks, and security expertise. That is what turns a capable model into an operational system.

The transferable business lesson reaches beyond cybersecurity. In finance, service, procurement, or IT operations, start by connecting the approved records, policies, and current workflow state that an experienced employee already consults. Then ask the AI to prepare the evidence, propose the next steps, and expose the uncertainty. A fluent answer without the right context may look productive while creating costly rework.

Automation Boundaries Are a Product Decision

Sophos gives MDR customers three response modes: Notify, where Sophos recommends a response and the customer acts; Collaborate, where they work together; and Authorise, where Sophos can respond on the customer’s behalf. The same boundaries apply whether the work is performed by a person or an agent. The company says anything it is not comfortable assigning to an agent is passed to human judgement.

That is a more practical governance question than “is the AI autonomous?” Every business should define which actions may be prepared automatically, which require a review, which need a customer or executive approval, and which are simply prohibited. The safest early wins are often in evidence gathering, triage, drafting, and execution of reversible, well-tested steps.

Measure Speed, Quality, and Exceptions Together

The 38-minute-to-89-second change is striking because it measures a real operating outcome: response time for cases using agents. Yet faster is only valuable if the classification is sound, the response is appropriate, and exceptions reach the right expert. Sophos' reported 52% end-to-end resolution rate should be read alongside its escalation boundary—not as a target to maximize at any cost.

For a pilot, track baseline and assisted cycle time, reviewer acceptance rate, false-positive and escalation rate, cost per resolved case, and the share of work that remains inside its approved boundary. Review a sample of finished cases every week. If error rates rise as coverage expands, narrow the agent’s authority or improve the inputs before chasing a larger automation percentage.

The Havlek Takeaway

Sophos' result suggests that the AI business case in cybersecurity is not “remove the analyst.” It is to let analysts spend less time assembling known evidence and more time handling the exceptions that demand expertise. Start with a clearly bounded case workflow, connect trusted operational context, make the human handoff explicit, and measure quality as rigorously as speed. That is how a large time-saving claim becomes a credible operating model.
